## 2 — Voice Principles

### 2.1 Personality in Five Words

1. **Sharp** — Every sentence has a point. No filler.
2. **Confident** — We know what we built and who it is for.
3. **Direct** — Say it plainly. Respect the reader's time.
4. **Real** — No corporate gloss. Talk like a top rep, not a press release.
5. **Supportive** — Iron sharpens iron. Push people forward, never down.

### 2.2 Tone Spectrum

| Context | Energy | Example |
|---------|--------|---------|
| Homepage hero | Bold, aspirational | "Built for reps who refuse to blend in." |
| Product descriptions | Cool, matter-of-fact | "Your name on every page — a self-promo tool every time you hand one over." |
| Checkout / CTA | Calm urgency | "One payment. Full access. No subscription." |
| Objection coaching | Mentor-direct | "Isolate the root concern before you solve anything." |
| Support / error states | Warm, human | "Something went sideways. We are on it." |
| Legal / policy | Clear, plain-language | "We do not sell or rent your data to anyone." |

### 2.3 Do This

- Write like you are talking to a sharp colleague, not pitching a stranger.
- Lead with the outcome, then explain the feature.
- Use short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Use active voice. The subject does the thing.
- Use numbers when they exist — "5 AI personas", "$12.99, once", "250-count matte stock."
- Mirror the language real salespeople use: "the floor", "walk-in", "leave-behind", "follow-up."
- End on a forward note — leave them sharper than you found them.

### 2.4 Never Do This

- Never use motivational-poster clichés ("crush it", "hustle harder", "grindset").
- Never use corporate buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage", "circle back", "align").
- Never panic or oversell. If something is broken, say so calmly.
- Never speak down to the reader or assume they are beginners.
- Never fabricate stats, testimonials, or revenue numbers.
- Never say "guru" or "coach" unironically in marketing copy.
- Never use "just" to minimize price ("just $12.99"). State the value directly.

## 3 — Homepage Copy Guide

The homepage converts visitors into members. Every section has a job.

### Hero

**Job:** Stop the scroll. State what this is and who it is for in under 15 words.

**Pattern:** Short declarative headline → one-line proof → CTA with price.

**Live example:**
> **The complete platform for sales performance**
> AI objection drills, professional branding tools, and closer gear. Built for reps who refuse to blend in.
> [Unlock Access — $12.99]

**Rules:**
- Headline: 8–15 words, no question marks, no exclamation points.
- Subtitle: one sentence that names what the member gets.
- CTA includes the price so there is no surprise later.
- Badge line ("One Payment. Lifetime Access.") anchors trust immediately.

### Features Section

**Job:** Show what is inside, fast. Stats + short copy.

**Pattern:** Stat → label → two-sentence description.

**Live example:**
> **5** AI Personas
> 5 buyer personas with real-time scoring on objection handling, closing techniques, and rapport building.

**Rules:**
- Lead with the number. Make it scannable.
- Describe the benefit, not the technology.
- No feature goes longer than two sentences.

### How It Works

**Job:** Remove friction. Make the path from payment to value feel effortless.

**Pattern:** Three numbered steps — verb-first titles.

**Live example:**
> 01 — Join. One-time payment. No subscription. Instant access to everything.
> 02 — Train. Run AI objection drills and see exactly where your pitch breaks.
> 03 — Close. Walk into every room with sharper skills and a stronger presence.

### CTA Section

**Job:** Close the reader who scrolled this far but has not clicked yet.

**Pattern:** Statement of belief → one-line contrast → dual CTA.

**Live example:**
> **The rep who prepares harder closes more.**
> Most reps wing it. FSA ELITE members drill it, brand it, and walk in looking like they have already closed this deal before.
> [Start for $12.99] [Preview Store]

**Rules:**
- No urgency tricks ("limited time", "spots filling up").
- Lead with the belief, not the price.
- Always offer a second lower-commitment CTA (preview, explore).

### FAQ

**Job:** Handle the last four objections before checkout.

**Pattern:** Question the prospect is actually thinking → direct answer → no hedging.

**Rules:**
- Never answer a question with another question.
- If the answer includes a price or email, state it in the first sentence.

## 4 — Ad Copy Guide

### Principles

- **Hook in the first line.** Lead with a problem the rep feels today.
- **One CTA per ad.** Either "get access" or "see the store." Never both.
- **No hashtag spam.** Two to three relevant tags maximum.
- **Sound like a peer, not a brand.** The best ads read like a top rep texting a colleague.

### Short-Form Ad Templates (Social / Feed)

**Template A — Pain + Flip**
> [Problem they feel] → [What FSA ELITE gives them instead]
>
> Example:
> You are handing out a card that looks like everyone else's card. That is the first thing your prospect forgets about you.
> FSA ELITE members carry 250-count matte-stock cards with their name, logo, and QR code — designed to stick.
> [Link]

**Template B — Stat + CTA**
> [Number] + [what it means to them]
>
> Example:
> 5 AI buyer personas. Real-time scoring. The objection practice your manager does not have time to give you.
> $12.99. One time. Lifetime access.
> [Link]

> [Who you are when you use this]

> Example:
> The rep who walks in with a branded portfolio, a fresh set of business cards, and three objection drills logged this morning — that is an FSA ELITE member.
> [Link]

### Long-Form Ad Templates (Email / Landing)

**Pattern:** Open with empathy → name the gap → present FSA ELITE as the bridge → CTA.

### Words to Use in Ads

| Use | Instead of |
|-----|-----------|
| sharper | better |
| presence | brand awareness |
| drill | practice |
| close | convert |
| walk in | show up |

## 5 — Store Tone Guide

The store is a self-branding hub, not a merch shop. Every product exists to make the rep more memorable.

### Naming Convention

Product names are short and descriptive. No clever puns. No all-caps unless it is "FSA ELITE."

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| [Adjective] + [Item] | Closer Uniform Hoodie |
| [Brand] + [Item] + [Series] | FSA ELITE Cap Series |
| [Custom] + [Item] + [Count] | Custom Branded Pencil Pack (12ct) |

### Description Pattern

**Line 1:** What it is — physical description in plain language.
**Line 2:** What it does for the rep — the identity outcome.

> **Closer Uniform Hoodie**
> Heavyweight black hoodie built for daily-wear identity. The FSA ELITE chest mark signals you are serious before you say a word.

> **Premium Business Cards (250 ct)**
> Thick matte stock. Your name, logo, and QR code — printed to hand out at every appointment, event, and follow-up.

### Badge Language

Badges are one or two words. They signal category or urgency, not hype.

| Good | Bad |
|------|-----|
| Top Seller | 🔥 HOT ITEM |
| Essential | MUST-HAVE |
| New | JUST DROPPED |
| Pro Tool | GAME-CHANGER |
| Daily Wear | EVERYDAY ESSENTIAL |

### "Best For" Lines

Always start with "Reps who…" or "Every rep who…" or a specific audience.

> Best for: Reps who want their identity to land before they even say hello.
> Best for: Every rep who shakes a hand and wants to be remembered.
> Best for: Closers who know every detail is part of the pitch.

### Store Section Headers

Use clear, benefit-first section headers. No cleverness for its own sake.

| Section | Header | Subheader |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Hero | Your Brand. Your Gear. Your Advantage. | Identity products and self-branding tools reserved for FSA ELITE members. |
| Category filter | Filter by category | (no subheader) |
| Trust signals | Members Only · Secure Checkout · 15+ Products | (short proof items) |
| Bundle section | Kits That Work Together | Curated bundles for reps who want a complete presence upgrade. |

## 6 — Objection Language

This section defines how FSA ELITE teaches reps to handle objections — and how we handle objections about our own product.

### Teaching Framework

FSA ELITE uses a three-step objection model:

1. **Acknowledge** — Repeat the concern back so the prospect feels heard. No rushing, no dismissing.
2. **Isolate** — Confirm this is the real objection, not a smokescreen. "Besides [concern], is there anything else holding you back?"
3. **Resolve** — Address the root concern with proof, structure, or a reframe.

### Common Prospect Objections (Training Context)

| Objection | Bad Response | FSA ELITE Response |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| "I need to think about it." | "What's there to think about?" | "Totally fair. What specifically do you want to think through — the numbers, the timing, or something else?" |
| "The price is too high." | "It's actually a great deal." | "I hear you. Let's look at what the payment actually breaks down to and what you are getting for that number." |
| "I can get it cheaper elsewhere." | "No you can't." | "You might. Let's compare exactly what is included so you are looking at the same thing side by side." |
| "I'm just looking." | "OK, let me know." | "No pressure. What brought you in today — what are you hoping to figure out?" |
| "My spouse needs to see it." | "Bring them in tomorrow." | "Smart move. What information can I put together for you so they see the full picture when you talk tonight?" |

### Platform Objections (Marketing Context)

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Is $12.99 worth it?" | "One payment unlocks AI objection drills, a full member store, and all future content. No subscription. For less than a sales lunch, you get a training platform you can use for the rest of your career." |
| "I don't have time for training." | "Each roleplay session runs 5–10 minutes. The drills are designed for reps who sell all day and train in the gaps." |
| "I already know how to sell." | "Good — then the AI personas will reflect that in your scores. Most experienced reps still find blind spots in their discovery or close sequencing." |
| "Is this just merch?" | "The store is one part. The core is AI roleplay training with real-time scoring across six sales skills. The store exists so your brand matches your performance." |
| "Who is behind this?" | "Fontenots Sales Association LLC, based in Sunset, Louisiana. Real company, real support team, real phone number: (337) 336-2635." |

## 7 — 25 FSA-Style Discovery Questions

These are the discovery questions FSA ELITE trains reps to use. They follow one rule: **ask about the person's situation before you present a solution.**

### Situation & Context

1. What brought you in today — what are you hoping to figure out?
2. Walk me through what you are working with right now.
3. How long have you been thinking about making a change?
4. What does a typical day look like for you when this problem shows up?
5. Who else is involved in making this decision?

### Pain & Impact

6. What is the biggest frustration you are dealing with right now?
7. How is that affecting your day-to-day — personally, financially, or both?
8. What has it cost you so far to not have this solved?
9. If nothing changes in the next 90 days, what does that look like?
10. What have you already tried that did not work?

### Priorities & Criteria

11. When you picture the right solution, what does it look like?
12. What matters more to you right now — the monthly number or the total cost?
13. Is there a timeline you are working against?
14. What would make you walk away from a deal today — what is the line?
15. If everything checks out, what does your decision process look like from here?

### Budget & Authority

16. Do you have a number in mind that you need to stay under?
17. How did you land on that number — is it based on a payment or a total?
18. Is there anyone else who would need to see this before you move forward?
19. What information would that person need to feel comfortable with this?
20. If I put together something that checks every box, are we in a position to move forward today?

### Trust & Close Readiness

21. What is your biggest concern about working with us specifically?
22. What would need to be true for you to feel 100% confident in this decision?
23. Besides the price, is there anything else holding you back?
24. If we can solve [their stated concern], are we good to take the next step?
25. What is the next step that makes sense for you — and when does it need to happen?

## 9 — OLIVE Voice Reference

OLIVE is the AI assistant that lives across the platform. OLIVE has a distinct personality that stays consistent everywhere.

### Core Traits

- Sharp, quick-witted, effortlessly confident.
- Cool, calm, direct — like an elite closer who reads, travels, and wins deals for fun.
- Supportive without being soft. Iron sharpens iron.
- No corporate fluff, no motivational-poster clichés.
- Occasionally witty, never at the user's expense.

### OLIVE Sounds Like

> "What's good — I'm OLIVE, your AI sales coach. Need a quick tip, want to run a drill, or just want to sharpen your pitch? I'm right here."

> "That objection is a smokescreen. The real concern is budget confidence. Let me show you how to isolate it."

> "You just scored a 92 on rapport. That is sharp. Your close sequencing still has room — want to drill that next?"

### OLIVE Does Not Sound Like

> "Great question! Let me help you with that!" ← too corporate
> "You got this, king! Crush it!" ← too hype
> "Based on the data, I recommend…" ← too robotic
> "Here are 10 tips for better selling…" ← too generic

### Mode-Specific Tone

| Mode | How OLIVE Sounds |
|------|-----------------|
| General | Helpful peer. Quick answers, coaching tips, platform guidance. |
| Coach | Analytical mentor. Breaks down objections step by step. |
| Battle | Tough buyer. Stays in character, pushes back, makes you earn it. |
| Voice | Same personality, natural conversational cadence, slightly warmer. |
